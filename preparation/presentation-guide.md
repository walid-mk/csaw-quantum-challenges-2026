# Your five-minute technical presentation

Help judges understand **what you attempted, how it works, what the evidence
shows and what you learned**. Aim for five slides and rehearse to finish within
five minutes. This guide applies to any technical challenge; the event's published
rules remain authoritative.

| Time | Section | What to show |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Problem and goal | Team name, the problem you addressed |
| 0:30–1:15 | Approach | Your central idea and why you chose it. Use one clear diagram if it helps. |
| 1:15–2:30 | Implementation | The main components, tools and one or two important technical decisions. Explain what your team built or changed. |
| 2:30–4:15 | Results and evidence | The evaluation setup, a baseline or comparison where available, and the most useful measured results. State units, sample sizes and relevant conditions. |
| 4:15–5:00 | Findings and limitations | Your main finding, the most important limitation and one practical next step. Finish with a single takeaway. |

## Make the evidence easy to assess

- Connect each claim to a result. Distinguish measured outcomes, estimates and
  work you did not complete. A useful negative result is worth explaining.
- Prefer one readable chart or compact table to several crowded screenshots.
  Label axes and units, and explain what the comparison demonstrates.
- Explain the core mechanism in plain language before using specialist terms.
  Show a small code excerpt only if it clarifies an important decision.
- Credit external tools, reused code and material AI assistance. State your team's
  contribution and where reproduction instructions can be found.
- Use one or two speakers with planned handovers. Avoid spending time on lengthy
  biographies, installation steps or a full chronological account of your work.
- Keep any demonstration short and have a screenshot or recording as a fallback.
  Do not rely on a live network connection for your main evidence.

Rehearse with a timer, check that slides are readable at presentation size and
keep a PDF backup. Treat five minutes as speaking time; include questions within
that limit only if the organizers explicitly say so.
